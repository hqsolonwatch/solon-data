MILE3

Districts

Some districts are drawn in shapes that stretch across a state to pick up some neighborhoods and leave out others. One common way to measure that is the Polsby-Popper score, which compares a district’s area to the length of its border. A perfect circle scores 1. The lower the score, the more stretched and irregular the shape.

District lines also decide who shares your representative. Everyone inside the lines votes in the same House race, so their priorities shape who wins and what that member works on. When a district stretches across very different places, one member answers to all of them. Solon Watch shows you which communities are in your district with you.

How it works

  1. Enter your address. Solon Watch finds your two senators, your House member and your district.
  2. Follow them. Each vote they cast shows up with a plain-English summary of the bill, next to where their campaign money came from.
  3. Get a free alert when a bill you care about is scheduled for a vote, and see how your representatives voted once it’s over.
  4. Send your senators and House member a message about a bill, before or after the vote.
MILE5

Where the numbers come from

Votes come from the official roll-call records on senate.gov and house.gov. Campaign totals come from the Federal Election Commission. PAC money comes from the contribution reports PACs file with the FEC, and each PAC’s type comes from its FEC registration. Lobbying comes from the federal lobbying disclosure database, where lobbyists list the bills they worked on. Area-by-area election results are certified results from each state’s election office. Some come straight from the state, and some come through OpenElections, a free public project that collects official results from state and local election offices.

Every number in Solon Watch shows the public record it came from. Solon Watch doesn’t endorse candidates or parties.
