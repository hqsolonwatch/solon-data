MILE1

The years in between

Congress votes most weeks, in election years and in between. We let you make your voice heard for the bills that you care about, and show you how your representative has voted in the past.

MILE2

How it works

  1. Enter your address. We find your two senators, your House member and your district.
  2. Tell us what issues you care most about.
  3. Get an alert when a bill you care about is scheduled for a vote, and see how your representatives voted once it’s over. Send them your opinions before and after any vote you care deeply about.
  4. Make an educated decision when it’s time to choose whether or not you want to send them back to Congress to represent you.
MILE3

Votes and money

We want to demystify money in politics. If your representative is not voting in a way that helps their constituents out, you should be able to see who else has a stake in their votes.

MILE4

Districts

We make finding representatives for any address easy, as well as seeing what areas any politician represents and how each county in their district voted. Here, we show two of the least compact districts in the country as an example of why you and your neighbor might have different representatives. The Polsby-Popper score compares a district’s area to the length of its border.

MILE5

Where the numbers come from

Votes come from the official roll-call records on senate.gov and house.gov. Campaign totals come from the Federal Election Commission. PAC money comes from the contribution reports PACs file with the FEC, and each PAC’s type comes from its FEC registration. Lobbying comes from the federal lobbying disclosure database, where lobbyists list the bills they worked on. Area-by-area election results are certified results from each state’s election office. Some come straight from the state, and some come through OpenElections, a free public project that collects official results from state and local election offices. The share of Americans who contacted an elected official comes from a Pew Research Center survey taken in September 2025.

We show the public record behind every number, and we don’t endorse candidates or parties.
