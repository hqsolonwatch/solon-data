The years in between

Bills get written and voted on, and campaigns keep raising money, long after Election Day. By the next election, most voters have lost track of what their representatives did. Solon Watch keeps that record in one place and tells you when they vote.

Districts

Some districts are drawn in shapes that stretch across a state to pick up some neighborhoods and leave out others. One common way to measure that is the Polsby-Popper score, which compares a district’s area to the length of its border. A perfect circle scores 1. The lower the score, the more stretched and irregular the shape.

It also matters who else lives in your district. Those neighbors share your representative and vote in the same House race, so their priorities help decide who wins and which issues get attention. District lines decide which communities are counted together. When a district stretches across very different places, one representative has to answer to all of them, and some voices can carry more weight than others. Solon Watch shows you who shares your district, so you can see whose votes count alongside yours.

How it works

  1. Enter your address. Solon Watch finds your two senators, your House member, and your district.
  2. Follow them. Each vote they cast shows up with a plain-English summary of the bill, next to where their campaign money came from.
  3. See which bills are coming up for a vote, and get a free alert when one you care about is scheduled. Afterward, see how your representatives voted.
  4. Tell them what you think. Solon Watch makes it easy to contact your senators and House member about a bill, before or after the vote.

Where the numbers come from

Votes come from the official roll-call records on senate.gov and house.gov. Campaign totals come from the Federal Election Commission, and industry totals from OpenSecrets, which sorts FEC donor records by industry. Lobbying comes from the federal lobbying disclosure database, where lobbyists list the bills they worked on. Area-by-area election results are certified results from each state’s election office. Some come straight from the state, and some come through OpenElections, a free public project that collects official results from state and local election offices.

Every number in Solon Watch shows the public record it came from. Solon Watch doesn’t endorse candidates or parties.
